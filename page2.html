A timeline on Gymnastics

The Ancient Times

Gymnastics has been around for centuries. Three notable background facts

  1. Practiced in ancient Greece, Rome, and Egypt
  2. Used to train soldiers for battle
  3. Focused on strength, agility, and flexibility

1800s

  • became more popular
  • Man named Friedrich Ludwig Jahn, nicknamed the "father of gymnastics", created the first gymnastics equipment
  • Jahn created the parallel bars and balance beams
  • Jahn founded the Turnverein movement in Germany

First modern Olympic gymnastics

  • included in first modern Olympic Games in Athens, Greece, 1896.
  • The events included rope climbing, parallel bars, horizontal bar, and vault.
  • Continues to be apart of the olympics today with the addition of new events and changes to the scoring system over time

Womens gymnastics in the Olympics

  • womens gymnastics first included in the Olympic Games in Amsterdam, Netherlands in 1928.
  • Events included rope climbing, balance beam, and team competition
  • Events have now expanded to included individual all-around scoring, vault, uneven bars, beam, and floor excercises

Development of modern gymnastics

  • The 1960s saw the rise of modern gymnastics, with new techniques and styles being developed
  • The introduction of foam pits and other safety equipment allowed gymnasts to attempt riskier manuevers, leading to the development of new sills and routines.

Gymnastics today

  • continues to be a popular sport worlwide with new talen emerging every year
  • For women, the four events are beam, vault, floor, and uneven bars where athletes perform for team and individual scores
  • The Internation Gymnastics Federation oversees the sport and sets the rules and regulations for competitions
